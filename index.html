Commentary

Central banks keep buying, with the East still leading

CreekRats Gold · 10 November 2026

The official sector's appetite for gold has held steady into autumn. Over the past twelve months central banks have added a net 452 tonnes, with 26 countries buying and 7 selling. World official holdings now stand at about 33,450 tonnes, and the number of countries holding gold has edged up to 121.

Poland remains the largest buyer over the past year, at 142 tonnes, while Ghana is the largest net seller at 14 tonnes. Smaller additions came from Jordan (about 5 tonnes), Kazakhstan (about 5 tonnes) and the Czech Republic (about 4 tonnes). The advanced economies, for the most part, stayed where they were.

China is again the name to watch. The latest official figures from the State Administration of Foreign Exchange show holdings rising to 78.3 million ounces in October, roughly 26 tonnes more than in September and a total of about 2,435 tonnes. That makes a further month of additions. These figures are provisional and will be replaced by the IMF's own when it publishes the month.

Russia's Finance Ministry has also announced a further month of budget-rule purchases of foreign currency and gold, at 312.6 billion roubles between 9 November and 4 December. The Ministry does not say how much of that is gold, so we track it as a flow and do not convert it into tonnes.

None of this should be over-read; a single month rarely settles anything and the official sector moves slowly by design. But the direction is consistent: central banks are still buying, and the accumulation continues to come from the emerging world.
